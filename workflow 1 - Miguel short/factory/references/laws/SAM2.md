# FIX ROUND 3

## 0. The one-line verdict

**SAM2 wins, but not for the reason the plan assumed.** It is not that the
tracked matte needs no temporal filtering — it still does, and the filter still
earns 23 %. It is that a *tracked* decision beats a *re-decided* one at the
tails: on frames where the picture is standing still, the round-2 matte's worst
single-frame edge jump is **7,024 px** and SAM2's is **2,925 px**, and inside the
head band it is **5,599 px vs 1,606 px** — 3.5× better where Morgane was looking.
Along the way it also removed a defect nobody had named: **round 2 was
amputating his hand**, up to 2,372 px of it, every time he raised it into the
chair's static exclusion region.

## 1. Why the model changes the shape of the problem

Round 1 and round 2 both run **BiRefNet-general per frame**. The model sees one
still image at a time and answers "which pixels are the person" from scratch,
1,354 times. Nothing in that pipeline knows that frame 700 is the same man in
the same chair as frame 699. Every stabilising device downstream — the S-curve
haze kill, keep-largest, the 3-frame temporal median, and in round 2 the whole
spatial exclusion mask — exists to paper over that fact after it has happened.

SAM 2 (Meta, Segment Anything 2) is a **video** segmenter: it carries a memory
bank of previous frames and *propagates* a decision made once. So the round-3
change is not "a better model", it is **moving the temporal reasoning from the
post stack into the model**, where it can use the picture instead of only the
previous masks.

**The plate is `_shared/face_wide_25.mp4`** — byte-identical to the bake-off's
`matte_bakeoff/src/canon_wide_25.mp4` (md5 `b4d776b3…`), 1080×900, 25 fps, 1354

Config `configs/sam2.1/sam2.1_hiera_b+.yaml` (base-plus; small was not needed —
CPU throughput turned out to be acceptable). `_C` fails to import because the
CUDA extension is not built; SAM2 prints a warning, skips one post-processing
step, and is otherwise fine on this footage.

### **MPS is broken here — CPU is not a fallback, it is the answer**

`torch.backends.mps.is_available()` returns True and the model runs without
error. **It returns noise.** The frame-0 probe on MPS produced a salt-and-pepper
mask over the entire frame: area 278,160 px scattered edge to edge, rightmost ON
pixel in the neck band at **x = 1079** (the frame's right edge). The identical
code with `SAM2_DEVICE=cpu` produced a clean single silhouette, area 362,299,
rightmost ON pixel **x = 721**. Proof image: `sam2/work/probe_f0.png` was
overwritten by the good run — the MPS numbers above are the record.

Nothing warns you. A silent-garbage backend is worse than a missing one, so
`SAM2_DEVICE` exists and CPU is what shipped.

**Cost of CPU:** 1,354 frames in **1,645 s (27.4 min), 1,215 ms/frame** on the
M5 Pro, ~310 % CPU, 6.4 GB resident. Entirely acceptable for a one-off plate.

### Chunking, and why it is not a compromise

The video predictor pre-allocates `N × 3 × 1024 × 1024` float32 — **17 GB for
the full take**, on a 48 GB machine running sibling agents. So the clip is walked
in **chunks of 350 frames with an 8-frame overlap**, and chunk *k+1* is
conditioned with `add_new_mask` on the mask chunk *k* produced for that exact
frame.

The overlap is not decoration: those 8 frames are decided twice, once by each
chunk, and the disagreement is the seam's error bar.

| seam at frame | pixel agreement, min over the 8 overlap frames |
|---|---|
| 350 | **99.666 %** |
| 700 | **99.810 %** |
| 1050 | **99.920 %** |

Improving across the take, because the handoff mask gets better as the memory
bank warms. The seams are not visible in any trace.

## 4. The post stack: what survived, what died, what is new

Round 2 (`cutout/cutout2_matte.py`) ran:

```
s-curve haze kill → SPATIAL EXCLUSION (chair) → keep-largest + fill-holes
→ exclusion re-applied → 3-frame temporal median → feather 0.6 → 7px cream rim
```

Round 3 (`sam2_ship.py`) runs:

```
0.5 cut → keep-largest + fill-holes → BORDER BLEED → 3-frame temporal median
→ feather 0.6 → 7px cream rim
```

**The S-curve is gone.** It existed to kill BiRefNet's translucent-chair haze —
"the dominant failure mode is not chair kept but chair kept at alpha 0.2–0.6"
(`mb_post.py`). SAM2 emits calibrated logits; the equivalent is a straight
sigmoid > 0.5. (SAM2's *interior* does carry a dithered 0.6–0.95 texture from
upsampling its low-res mask — binarising at 0.5 removes it, which is why the
alpha is binary-cored and soft only at the boundary.)

**The spatial exclusion mask is DEAD, and deleting it fixed a bug** — §6.

**Keep-largest + fill-holes stay.** They cost nothing on a clean single blob and
they are the cheap insurance against a stray component.

**Feather 0.6 and the 7 px cream `#FFFDF9` rim are unchanged.** Miguel confirmed
the die-cut rim in round 2; nothing here touches its look.

### New: BORDER BLEED (8 px, three edges)

SAM2 attenuates its mask **into the frame border**; BiRefNet does not. Measured
on frame 0, ON pixels per row:

| row | 880 | 885 | 890 | 893 | 896 | 898 | 899 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| BiRefNet | 1068 | 1069 | 1070 | 1071 | 1071 | 1072 | **1072** |
| SAM2 raw | 1058 | 1062 | 1063 | 1062 | 622 | 0 | **0** |

His chest genuinely runs off the bottom of the plate, so a mask that fades out
over the last five rows is an upsampling artifact, not a statement about the
subject. Left alone it opens a transparent strip under him **and the 7 px rim
then draws a cream line across the bottom**, turning the die-cut into a floating
sticker.

Fix: the outer 8 px of the **left, right and bottom** edges inherit the nearest
interior column/row. Where that interior line is OFF — which is most of the left
and right edge — the OR changes nothing, so this cannot invent subject anywhere.
Measured cost on frame 0: **+3,636 px, all inside y ∈ [858, 899]**. The **top
edge is deliberately excluded**: his cap sits mid-frame and must stay honest.

### The temporal median: measured, and it stays

The brief predicted the median would be "nearly unnecessary". **It is not.**
Both variants were traced over all 1,354 frames before anything was rendered
(`sam2_ship.py --measure` → `median_decision.json`):

| | SAM2, no median | SAM2, 3-frame median |
|---|---|---|
| area Δ std | 2,858.8 | **1,137.4** |
| right-extent Δ std | 12.19 | **7.45** |
| frame-to-frame IoU, mean | 0.99333 | **0.99475** |
| frame-to-frame IoU, min | 0.82031 | **0.96259** |
| frames below IoU 0.990 | 160 | **107** |

And on the still-frame flicker metric that matters (§5), the median buys
**959 vs 1,241 px** of mean edge motion — **23 %**. Its *job* changed (it is no
longer suppressing gross re-decisions, it is smoothing boundary quantisation)
but it has not become free to drop. **Shipped with `--temporal 3`.**

## 5. Stability: the measurement, including the one I got wrong first

### The wrong metric, and why it is wrong

The obvious approach — per-frame silhouette area and left/right extent, then
diff them — produced a ranking where SAM2 looked *worse* than round 2 (right Δ
max 240 px vs 117 px). Every one of its "worst" frames was decoded: **10, 246,
951, 1097, 1138.** Every one is **his hands entering or leaving frame**
(`work/glitches.png`). A silhouette that correctly grows by 67,697 px when two
hands come up is not flickering, it is tracking. And round 2's right-extent
trace is *artificially* calm because its exclusion rectangle physically clamps
that edge.

So the raw traces are reported below for completeness, but they are contaminated
and they are **not** the verdict.

| matte | area Δ std | right Δ std | right Δ max | left Δ std | IoU mean | IoU min | frames < 0.990 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| BiRefNet raw (round-1 model out) | 4,575.6 | 14.74 | 159 | 9.60 | 0.98877 | 0.91204 | 527 |
| round-2 SHIPPED | 1,404.1 | 5.92 | 117 | 8.39 | 0.99462 | 0.96079 | 133 |
| SAM2 raw (no post at all) | 2,580.2 | 12.31 | 240 | 7.91 | 0.99288 | 0.83318 | 198 |
| **SAM2 SHIPPED (median 3)** | **1,137.4** | 7.45 | 143 | **7.31** | **0.99475** | **0.96259** | **107** |

### The right metric: **matte motion without picture motion**

Flicker is the trim moving when the picture is not. So: compute the mean |Δluma|
between consecutive plate frames, take the **quietest 20 % — 271 frames**
(threshold 1.381 luma levels, against an all-frame mean of 2.380), and on exactly
those frames measure the **XOR area between consecutive masks**: how many pixels
changed their mind while the man was still.

This is what makes the metric safe from the hands problem: on a frame where the
whole picture moved by ≤ 1.38 luma levels, **his hands did not move either**, so
XOR cannot be charged to legitimate tracking. It is edge motion or it is
nothing. A second column repeats the measurement over the **head band, rows
150–560** — the cap-and-face zone, above where the hands spend most of the take —
as a cross-check. (It is *not* claimed that no hand ever enters rows 150–560; a
gesture can reach y ≈ 320. The band is applied identically to all four mattes,
so whatever it includes, it includes for everyone.)

`sam2_flicker.py --still` → `flicker_still.json`, 271 frames:

| matte | XOR mean | XOR p95 | XOR max | head XOR mean | head XOR p95 | head XOR max |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| BiRefNet raw | 3,508.4 | 13,281 | 25,418 | 1,550.0 | 7,116 | 16,417 |
| round-2 SHIPPED | 1,187.8 | 2,839 | 7,024 | 575.1 | 1,086 | 5,599 |
| SAM2, no median | 1,240.8 | 2,112 | 5,183 | 658.6 | 1,243 | 1,622 |
| **SAM2 SHIPPED (median 3)** | **959.0** | **1,845** | **2,925** | **530.2** | 1,112 | **1,606** |

**SAM2 shipped wins on every column but one.**

* mean edge motion on still frames **1,188 → 959 px, −19 %**
* p95 **2,839 → 1,845 px, −35 %**
* **worst single frame 7,024 → 2,925 px, 2.4× better**
* **worst single frame in the head band 5,599 → 1,606 px, 3.5× better**

The tails are the story. A shimmer is not perceived as an average; it is
perceived as the frames where the edge lurches. Round 2's worst still-frame
lurch in the head band is 5,599 px — that is Morgane's complaint expressed as a
number, and it is the number that fell by 3.5×.

The one column round 2 wins is head XOR p95 (1,086 vs 1,112 px, 2 %), which is
inside the noise of everything else here and does not survive the max column.

Note also that even **SAM2 with no temporal filter at all** beats the fully
filtered round-2 stack on both tails (p95 2,112 vs 2,839; max 5,183 vs 7,024).
That is the tracked decision doing the work, before any post.

## 6. The defect nobody had reported: round 2 was cutting his hand off

Round 2's chair exclusion is a **static region** — 58,499 px derived from
"dark AND still AND rarely-matted", including a 156 × 345 rectangle at (692, 321)
over the right headrest wing. It is subtracted from every frame unconditionally.

**His hand goes there.** He gestures with his hands up beside his face for much
of the take, and when a hand crosses that rectangle, round 2 subtracts it.

Checked directly: take the pixels SAM2 keeps that fall inside round-2's
exclusion region (eroded 8 px, so neither build is charged for its own
antialiased edge) and ask the **plate** what colour they are
(`sam2_flicker.py --skin` → `exclusion_skin.json`):

| frame | px SAM2 keeps in the region | mean BGR | mean luma | skin % | dark % | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10 | 1,328 | 91, 90, 147 | 107.6 | **100.0** | 0.0 | his hand |
| 1097 | 2,372 | 90, 87, 142 | 104.3 | **92.1** | 4.0 | his hand |
| 1138 | 1,383 | 121, 120, 177 | 137.7 | **99.2** | 0.7 | his hand |
| 951 | 299 | 46, 50, 74 | 57.4 | 43.1 | 56.9 | hand edge |
| 303 | 191 | 8, 9, 13 | 10.2 | 1.0 | 91.1 | chair sliver |
| 580 | 32 | 2, 2, 3 | 2.9 | 0.0 | 100.0 | chair sliver |

So the answer to "does SAM2 leak chair where round 2 masked it" is: **on the
worst sampled frames it keeps 1,328–2,372 px that are 92–100 % skin — a hand
round 2 amputated — and at most 191 px that are genuinely chair.** For scale,
round 1 leaked **4,448 px/frame** of chair through the same region, and round 2
bought its zero by cutting through whatever else was standing there.

Visual proof: `sam2/work/exclusion_vs_hand.png` — magenta outlines the exclusion
region, orange is round 2, green is SAM2. At f10, f1097, f1137, f1138 the orange
contour visibly carves a notch out of a raised hand; green wraps it.

The chair-exclusion mask is therefore **not carried into round 3.** It is not
that it stopped being needed, it is that it was costing more than it bought.

## 9. Honest verdict

**Ship it.** The tracked matte is better where it was asked to be better:
worst-case trim motion on still frames falls 2.4× overall and 3.5× in the head
band, mean falls 19 %, and the chair is excluded by a prompt rather than by a
rectangle that also ate his hand.

**Three things this does not claim.**

1. **It is not median-free.** The plan expected the temporal filter to become
   unnecessary; it still buys 23 % of the mean and 44 % of the worst case (5,183 -> 2,925 px),
   and it shipped. What SAM2 removed is the *exclusion mask*, not the median.
2. **On raw per-frame area/extent diffs, round 2 still looks calmer** (right Δ
   std 5.92 vs 7.45). That comparison is not fair in either direction — round 2's
   rectangle clamps the very edge being measured, and SAM2's number is inflated
   by hands it is correctly tracking — but the raw table is above rather than
   omitted, because the fair-looking number is not always the flattering one.
3. **It is CPU-only on this machine** until the MPS backend stops returning
   noise. 27 minutes per 54-second plate is fine for a lab and would not be fine
   for a nightly factory run.

**Not tried, and worth trying if this ever needs to be faster or better:** the
`sam2.1_hiera_large` checkpoint (base-plus was enough, so large was never
justified); re-prompting with fresh negatives at chunk boundaries instead of the
pure mask handoff (the seams already agree to 99.7 %, so there was nothing to
fix); and multi-object tracking with the chair as its own tracked object, which
would give a subtractive matte instead of a prompted negative.

**Morgane's other cutout note — "would shine with a REAL screen recording behind
him" — is untouched here.** It is a staging idea, not a matte one.

## v2.1 The flicker: where it actually is

§5's still-frame XOR is the right *idea* and the wrong *resolution*. Its head
band is 410 rows tall, and one number over 410 rows cannot see a 45-row defect —
which is exactly why v1 shipped with this in it.

So `sam2_capband.py` builds a **per-row edge trace** instead: for every frame and
every row from 150 to 600, the leftmost and rightmost ON pixel of the shipped
mask. 1,354 × 450 × 2 int16 = 2.4 MB, cached once, and every number in this
section is a slice of that one array.

The metric per band is the **|Δ| of the band's mean row extent between
consecutive frames**, restricted to the same quietest 20 % of frames §5 uses
(271 frames, threshold 1.381 luma) so a moving hand can never be charged to the
trim. The band mean rather than a per-row max, because the defect is a whole
45-row sliver switching on and off together and averaging the rows is what
separates that from the ±1 px quantisation every band has.

## v2.3 The fix — and why it is a MASK prompt, not clicks

The brief said clicks first, and clicks were tried first. **They fail
structurally.** SAM2 re-segments a prompted frame from the clicks it is given;
six points describing a 14 px corridor describe a 14 px object. The 120-frame
smoke run came back with a **29,518 px hollow ribbon** instead of a 360,000 px man
(`smoke_v2.log`, and the raw probe in that run). Points are only safe on frame 0,
where the whole silhouette is specified by the other thirteen.

SAM2 takes a **mask** prompt at any frame — the chunk handoff in §2 already uses
one — and a mask specifies the whole object. So each corrective prompt is the v1
mask at that keyframe with rows 303-367 re-cut to the measured edge and nothing
else touched:

* the edge is measured per row over rows 303-367, gaps filled by a robust line
  fit through the rows that measured cleanly — 28 to 46 of the 65 per keyframe —
  because the cap's side seam is very close to straight (measured slope +0.04 to
  −0.26 px/row across all 21), then lightly smoothed;
* for x ≥ 655, the row is set ON up to that edge and OFF for the next 60 px;
* past 60 px nothing is touched, so a raised hand out at x > edge+60 survives.

Cost per keyframe: **+135 to +1,014 px added, 0 to 222 removed**
(`cap_prompts.json`). Visual before/after: `work/capfix_before_after.png`.

**21 keyframes** — one at or just before the start of each of the 17 windows,
plus fillers so no stretch of the take runs more than 125 frames without one:
0, 90, 160, 232, 275, 400, 460, 530, 585, 650, 720, 780, 845, 900, 960, 1030,
1100, 1170, 1230, 1290, 1340.

    780,845,900,960,1030,1100,1170,1230,1290,1340

## v2.5 The trim lines

v1's tail was `median → Gaussian 0.6 → threshold`, i.e. a whole-pixel staircase
softened by half a pixel, and the 7 px cream rim was dilated **from that
staircase**, repeating every jaggy at 7 px offset. A smooth silhouette with a
stepped rim would have been pointless, so both are rebuilt together.

`sam2_smooth.polish`, one pass at 2×:

```
binary → denub(open+close, k=2) → NEAREST ×2 → Gaussian(σ=4.0) → >0.5
       → INTER_AREA ×½ → Gaussian(0.55)
```

Upsampling *before* the blur is what makes it sub-pixel: thresholding at 2×
places the boundary on a half-pixel lattice, and INTER_AREA back to 1× turns that
into a genuine 1 px anti-aliased edge rather than a blurred hard one. The rim is
then dilated **in the same 2× space** from the same smoothed silhouette
(`ell(7×2)`, same low-pass), so the cream outline inherits the curves.

| parameter | value | native equivalent |
|---|---|---|
| `UP` | 2 | half-pixel lattice |
| `SIGMA_HI` | **4.0** | 2.0 px |
| `MORPH_K` | 2 | removes nubs / notches under 5 px |
| `FEATHER` | 0.55 px | final 1× Gaussian |
| rim | 7 px, dilated at 2× | unchanged from v1 |

**σ was chosen by eye at 10× on the brim notch, the ear and the shoulder over
frames 400 and 1100** (`work/smooth_sweep_*.png`): at 2.0 the stairs are still
legible, at 3.0 faint, at **4.0 gone with the brim notch still a square corner**,
at 5.0 the notch begins to bevel, at 6.0 it is a blob.

**Miguel is bald**, so — contrary to the original brief — there is no fine organic
texture anywhere on this trim to protect; cap fabric, ears, skin and t-shirt are
all smooth in reality, and the smoothing is harder than a haired subject would
allow. The two features that did constrain σ are the **cap brim corner** (a real
hard corner) and the **ear contour**. Both survive at 4.0.

The cost is bounded and measured, not assumed: at σ 4.0 the largest connected
group of pixels that changes anywhere in the frame is **9 px with a 5×3 bounding
box** — nothing on this silhouette moves more than about 2 px.

The edge itself, row 400 of frame 0, alpha across the boundary:

```
v1:  1.000  1.000  0.998  0.860  0.308  0.170  0.140  0.030
v2:  1.000  1.000  1.000  0.965  0.695  0.180  0.016  0.000
```

v1 has a lumpy tail with a 0.17/0.14 plateau — that is the staircase leaking
through the blur. v2 is monotonic over about 2 px. Partial-alpha pixels per
frame fall 6,865 → 5,740: a tighter edge, not a softer one.

**The number.** Perimeter is useless here (an 8-connected staircase and a 45°
line have nearly the same arc length — it moved 1 %). So: recover the iso-0.5
line at 4× with a bicubic resample, walk it, and report the mean distance from
each point to a 9-tap circular moving average of itself. **Measured on the alpha,
never on a thresholded copy** — v2's gain is sub-pixel, and re-quantising before
measuring reports −12.8 % for a change that is unmissable at 10×.

| roughness (px off its own local mean) | v1 | v2 | |
|---|---|---|---|
| whole contour | 0.0556 | **0.0353** | **−36.5 %** |
| head region only | 0.0773 | **0.0530** | **−31.4 %** |

## v2.7 Re-composite: `Fixed3/cutout_fix3b.mp4`

`cutout3b_matteswap.sh`: one substitution, `matte_sam2_rim.webm` →
`matte_sam2_rim_v2.webm`, and **`cutout3_envelope.json` is deliberately NOT
re-measured**. The brief is a matte swap, not a new layout, and re-measuring would
move lane gutters and occlusion clearances. That is defensible because the v2
silhouette differs from v1 by ~15 px only inside rows 305-365 on the image right
and by under 2 px everywhere else.

**The composition is provably identical.** The matte path in the composition is
the constant `assets/v/matte.webm`, so a matte swap cannot touch the HTML — and
`index.html` regenerates **byte-identical**, verified twice (once as a pre-flight
with the v1 matte, once on the real build).

Proving the *render* changed nothing else took three tries and the first two were
the check's fault, not the render's:

1. "every differing pixel must be in the matte-alpha-disagreement zone" — failed
   at ~10 k px/frame. **Neither the matte webm nor the output mp4 is lossless.**
   Control: v1-rim against v1-rimless, whose colour planes are the identical
   plate, differ inside their shared opaque area by mean 1.96 levels and **6,893
   pixels above tolerance 8**. Codec noise is not a composition change.
2. So: a **null control**, `out/_null_control.mp4`, the same composition rendered
   twice. (It nearly was not one — `stage_assets` only re-copies the matte when
   the staged file is *older*, so staging v1 over a newer v2 silently did nothing
   and the "v1 null" was a second v2 render. Caught by md5. Still a valid null,
   and the ship script now deletes the staged matte first so it cannot recur.)
3. And the verdict is on **amplitude, not count**. Counting cannot separate "the
   encoder allocated bits differently because the bottom half changed" from
   "something moved"; how *wrong* each pixel is can. This composition is white
   type on a pale ground — a 1 px shift would differ by 100-200 levels.

`cutout3_matteswap_check.py --frames 120`:

| region | swap, px/frame | null, px/frame | ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| **above the matte** (rows 0-1019: every caption, lane, tile, meter, logo) | 96.0 | 34.4 | 2.8× |
| matte edge zone | 3,706.1 | 47.5 | **78×** |
| matte interior | 9,704.7 | 177.6 | 55× |

| worst single pixel above the matte | levels /255 |
|---|---|
| the swap | **58** |
| the null control (identical file, re-rendered) | **57** |

**PASS.** Above the matte the swap is indistinguishable from re-rendering the
same file, so no composition element moved. The edge zone carries 78× the null —
that is the change. The interior is the matte webm's own re-encode, the same
order as the §1 control.

## v2.8 Honest verdict

**Ship it.** The one thing Miguel could still see is gone: the cap/chair sliver's
worst still-frame jump falls 4.2× and its nine ≥ 6 px events fall to zero, with no
control band anywhere on the head regressing. The trim is 31-37 % closer to a
smooth curve and the rim now inherits those curves instead of repeating the
stairs. The whole-silhouette flicker metric improves in every column including
the one round 2 still held.

**Four things this does not claim.**

1. **The clicks route was not made to work, it was abandoned.** A mask prompt is
   a legitimate SAM2 prompt and it is what shipped, but "corrective clicks at
   keyframes" as the brief imagined it is recorded here as *tried and structurally
   wrong*, not as untried.
2. **The corrections are seeded from v1.** Each keyframe mask is the v1 mask with
   one band rewritten, so a v1 error outside rows 303-367 at a keyframe would be
   propagated forward rather than fixed. The band tables say there was none to
   propagate, but the dependency is real.
3. **The measured-edge detector assumes the three-regime plate.** It refuses rows
   where cap→chair→wall is not clean (it found 28-46 usable rows out of 65 per
   keyframe and fitted the rest). On a different set, a different chair, or a
   lighter cap, it would need re-deriving, not re-using.
4. **It is still CPU-only and now slower** — 32.2 min against v1's 27.4, because
   21 conditioning frames cost memory-bank attention. Fine for a lab, not for a
   nightly run.

**Not tried:** re-deriving the envelope on the v2 silhouette (deliberately out of
scope here, and worth doing if v2 becomes the standing matte); tracking the chair
as a second SAM2 object, which would make the cap/chair boundary a *competition*
between two tracked objects instead of a prompted correction, and is the
principled version of this fix.

## v3.3 The mechanism: the memory bank, and the model's own testimony

The measured edge is computed from the plate alone. Comparing it to the v2 mask
every sixtieth frame across the take (`sam2_leftprompt.py --check`) gives the
whole story in one column:

| frames | v2 mask − measured edge |
|---|---|
| f0 - f660 | **+4.5 to +14.8 px** (mean ≈ **+10.5**) |
| f720 - f900 | −3.1 to +5.7 px (mean ≈ **+1.4**) |
| f960 - f1320 | −3.7 to +2.0 px (mean ≈ **−0.1**) |

SAM 2 carries a **memory bank** of previously-seen frames and attends to it when
deciding the current one. At t = 0 that bank holds one conditioning frame and
almost no history; by f1000 it holds the whole preceding take. On an edge with
plenty of contrast this does not matter. On an edge the picture cannot resolve —
black cap against a dark chair wing — it is the only evidence there is, and the
early frames do not have it. **The trim is worse at the beginning because the
model knows less at the beginning.**

**And the target is not this document's opinion.** Over the final third — the part
Miguel calls "a LOT better" — the v2 mask agrees with the plate-measured edge to

> **+0.08 px mean, sd 1.83 px, over 14 sampled frames**

i.e. the edge v3 injects is *exactly the one the propagation converges to on its
own once it has enough history*. v3 does not impose a new answer; it hands the
model, on frame 0, the answer it would otherwise take 26 seconds to earn.

### Why not backward propagation

The obvious alternative — propagate from a late, settled frame and blend the
reverse pass into the early section — was rejected on this evidence. It buys the
early frames a warm bank, but it also re-decides everything else, it needs a
blend seam somewhere in the middle of a shot with no cut, and it cannot be
verified band-by-band the way a keyframe can. The keyframe route is v2's, it is
already proven on this footage, and it is auditable one frame at a time.

## v3.4 The fix

Same technique as v2 — **mask prompts, never clicks** (§v2.3 records why points
fail structurally: SAM2 re-segments a prompted frame from the points it is given,
and a 14 px corridor of points describes a 14 px object) — with two changes.

**1. The target is regime-aware, per row.** `sam2_leftprompt.target_on_row`
decides from the plate which measurement the local physics supports, rather than
from a row number:

* the 12 px immediately left of the cap are **wall** (≥ 150) → the edge is the
  **50 % crossing** of the wall→cap step;
* they are darker than that → it is the **chair**, there is no usable ramp, and
  the edge is where **cap-black begins**, which is v2's right-hand rule.

Rows supporting neither are filled by a robust line fit through the rows that
did, then lightly smoothed — again v2's construction. Per keyframe the detector
measured **21 to 40 of the 47 band rows**, split 0-37 wall-kind and 2-37
chair-kind depending on how his head sits, with fitted slopes between −0.20 and
+0.16 px/row.

**2. The keyframes are front-loaded, and each re-asserts BOTH sides.** 24 of
them, dense where the error is:

```
early  (f0-451, 13)   0  40  80  120  140  160  200  240  280  320  360  400  440
middle (f452-902, 7)  500  565  630  695  760  825  890
final  (f903-1353, 4) 1000  1110  1220  1330
```

Each mask is **the v2 mask** with rows 322-368 re-cut to the measured LEFT edge
*and* rows 303-367 re-cut to v2's measured RIGHT edge (`sam2_capprompt.edge_profile`,
33-45 of 65 rows measured). Re-asserting the right costs nothing and removes a
real hazard: a v3 keyframe that happened to land on a bad right-side frame would
otherwise freeze that state into the bank. Cost per keyframe **+90 to +791 px
added, 1 to 241 removed** (`left_prompts.json`); the added count falls with the
frame index, which is the defect measured a third way.

## v3.9 Honest verdict

**Ship it.** The residue Miguel could still see is gone and, more usefully, it is
*explained*: the trim was worse at the start because SAM2's memory bank is thin at
the start, and on a black-cap-against-dark-chair edge the memory is the only
evidence available. Front-loading measured mask prompts removes the warm-up. In
the defect band the early third's worst still frame falls 4.3× and now equals the
final third's, its six ≥3 px events fall to zero, and no control band anywhere on
the silhouette regresses.

**Five things this does not claim.**

1. **The early/final gradient is not fully abolished** — only in the band that was
   defective. The whole-silhouette still-frame XOR is still ~20 % noisier early
   than late, in v3 as in v2, and §v3.1 argues that residue is the cap crown's
   ill-conditioned extent metric rather than a visible defect. If Miguel still
   sees something at the start, that is the next thing to measure.
2. **Two p95 columns regressed by 1.6-2.3 %** (§v3.6). The explanation offered —
   a genuinely larger silhouette has more boundary to quantise — is consistent
   with the area table and with every mean and max improving, but it is an
   explanation, not a proof.
3. **The corrections are seeded from v2, which was seeded from v1.** Each keyframe
   is the v2 mask with two bands rewritten, so an error outside rows 303-368 at a
   keyframe propagates forward rather than being fixed. Three passes of this now
   sit on top of one another; the band tables say there was nothing to propagate,
   but the dependency is real and it compounds.
4. **The regime detector assumes this set.** It refuses rows where wall→chair→cap
   is not clean (21-40 usable of 47 per keyframe) and fits the rest. A different
   chair, a lighter cap or a different key light needs it re-derived, not re-used.
5. **Still CPU-only, and slightly slower again** — 33.2 min against v2's 32.2 and
   v1's 27.4, because 24 conditioning frames cost more memory-bank attention than
   21. Fine for a lab, not for a nightly run.

**Not tried:** backward propagation from a settled late frame with the early
section blended from the reverse pass (rejected on the reasoning in §v3.3 — it
re-decides everything to fix one band, and cannot be audited per band); tracking
the chair as a second SAM2 object, which would make **both** cap/chair boundaries
a competition between two tracked objects instead of two prompted corrections,
and remains the principled version of v2's and v3's fix; and re-deriving
`cutout3_envelope.json` on the v3 silhouette, still deliberately out of scope and
still worth doing if v3 becomes the standing matte.

# STANDING RULE — FRAME 0 (Miguel, 2026-08-31, from the airtable cutout port)

**A frame-0 point prompt may never be a frame that ships.** §v2.3 already recorded
that points fail *after* frame 0 because SAM2 re-segments a prompted frame from
the prompt it is handed. The airtable port measured the other half of that
sentence: **they fail ON frame 0 too, for the same reason.** A prompted frame is a
from-scratch segmentation off dots while every other frame in the run is a
memory-conditioned propagation, so frame 0 arrives with a visibly different — and
rougher — contour than frame 1. On the airtable plate, edge roughness at frame 0
(RMS deviation of the boundary from its own sigma-3 smoothed self, rows 100-640,
measured on the shipped `matte_*_rim.webm`):

| | right edge | left edge |
|---|---|---|
| 14-point frame-0 prompt (shipped) | **1.09 px** | **0.87 px** |
| after the fix below | **0.58 px** | **0.62 px** |

On the raw track the anomaly is starker still: frame 0's right-edge curvature was
**1.385** against **0.545-0.589** for frames 2-5 — the opening frame was 2.4x
rougher than its own neighbours, on the one frame that decides whether anyone
watches.

## The three parts, and which one does the work

**1. WARM-UP LAP — the fix.** Build chunk 0's frame list as

    [ f0 ] + [ f15, f14, … f1 ] + [ f0, f1, f2, … ]

prompt local index 0, and **emit from local index 16**. The prompt lands on a
COPY of frame 0 that is thrown away; the real frame 0 then arrives as a
propagation with a 16-frame memory bank behind it, exactly like frame 300. The
16 warm-up masks are computed and discarded. §v3.3's finding — *"the trim is
worse at the beginning because the model knows less at the beginning"* — is the
whole argument; the lap is the cheapest way to let it know something first.

**2. MIRRORED PAD on the temporal median.** The post stack padded the 3-frame
median by REPLICATION, so output 0's window was `[f0, f0, f1]` and two of three
votes were the same frame. A binary median with a duplicated vote *is* that vote:
**frame 0 was the only frame in the video the smoother never touched.** Mirrored

* The wing is removable from the plate's own measurement

  inside the wing rows). **The fringe is not.** Bounding the prompt to the

x 275-304   lift_mean 13.1   dark 0.980
            persistence 0.604   (floor 0.75)      sd_in 12.04   (ceiling 12.0)

Neither rejection is a threshold problem. **Every statistic was computed over the
whole video**, and the tab stands for 0.585 of it — so persistence reads 0.604,
and the sd is inflated by the tab *ending* (top-y ~516 for 28 s, ~533 after: a

1. **windowing** — persistence, wander, darkness and the run detection itself all
